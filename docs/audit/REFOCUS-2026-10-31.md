## 0. Executive summary

1. **The engine still doesn't exist.** Real pieces now exist: the HMM
   trainer, Kneser-Ney LM, fabric decision contract, lattice format,
   why-view and correction facts. The thing that turns audio into words
   with them (R5 decoder) has not been started, and neither have
   A2/A5/A6. Most of Oct 28–31 went into the 30-word A1 gate and three
   recorder rewrites.
2. **Recording drift is real, and the cause is one decision.** Owner
   audio came from two capture chains: Windows/WASAPI (run1, clean) and
   WSL/Pulse (every later session: two muted-take incidents, 5–99% sample
   burst overflow, one VOID protocol). Fix: **Windows chain only, the
   existing recorder plus two small changes, and one canonicalizer that
   every experiment reads through** (RS-1).
3. **The A1 "length mismatch" is likely an endpointer defect.** The
   trimmer keeps anything above −40 dB re peak. The committed endpoint
   audit shows run1 speech "starting" 0–20 ms into the clip on 12/12
   clips, which points to the Enter-key click or stream-start transient.
   A 10-minute check confirms or kills this before any more recording.
4. **The training data is the real bottleneck, not code.** The SI acoustic
   model was trained on ~23 minutes of courtroom audio (PER 0.85, WER
   1.44). The LM has 37k tokens. Neither can support a transcriber. Fix:
   approve **LibriSpeech train-clean-100** (100 h human read speech,
   CC BY 4.0, free, no account) and the **LibriSpeech LM text corpus**
   (public-domain books), plus your own writing as the personal LM.
5. **Several "GREEN" results are machinery checks, not evidence:**
   - R4's adaptation "win" is PER 1.80 → 1.67, where PER > 1 means
     garbage output;
   - the enrollment phase-test used Supreme Court audio for owner
     adaptation;
   - the CI method is a normal approximation, not the planned paired
     bootstrap.
   Section 3 relabels these.
6. **Model:** Muse Spark 1.3 executes tight beads well and reports
   honestly. It fails at judgment: it rebuilds instead of reusing,
   patches symptoms, accepts nonsense metrics as green, and loads
   ceremony onto small questions. **Put a stronger model in the
   architect/reviewer seat; keep Spark on lanes.**
7. **Nov 4 is reachable for a real demo** if work splits into the 7 lanes
   in §6 today: file and live transcription of your read speech by a
   LibriSpeech-trained classical recognizer with LM, per-word "why", one
   correction loop, and honest WER. A6 and triphones are stretch goals.
   Open vocabulary and daily-driver accuracy are not Nov 4 goals.

## 2. Root causes of the drift

1. **Two capture chains.** run1 = Windows WASAPI fifine 48 kHz (clean).
   Every later session = WSL Pulse at 16 kHz: muted takes (2/2 sessions),
   burst overflow (`record_exact` truncates to the *first* N samples, so
   if the excess is pre-roll backlog, the *end* of the utterance is what
   gets cut), different noise floor, different onset behaviour. Cross-
   session A1 then compared two microphones-plus-channels, not two takes.
2. **Recorder rewrites instead of reuse.** Oct 28 lane told to "write ONE
   new file" → `record_phase_test.sh` (VOID protocol) → sealed mode →
   continuous mode. The owner's working recorder was bypassed twice.
3. **Endpointer accepts non-speech.** `TRIM_K 0.01` (−40 dB re peak) +
   `TRIM_FLOOR 0.001`. The audit checked truncation, not tightness, so a
   leading click/transient passed as speech (lead 0–20 ms on all audited
   clips). Cross-session lengths diverge because the click is
   chain-dependent.
4. **Symptom patching.** Each failure got a new bead that guards the
   symptom (silence gate, overflow truncation, length-robust DTW `8lx`).
   Nobody asked "same chain? same endpoint?" first.
5. **Ceremony outran substance.** Seals, precommit hashes, score-once
   and quarantine for a 30-word DTW check consumed five owner sessions.
   Meanwhile the decoder that every product feature depends on was never
   started.
6. **Metric sanity not enforced.** "GREEN" was granted to PER > 1, WER >
   1, a 23-minute acoustic model, and an owner-adaptation test scored on
   Supreme Court audio. Gates checked that numbers were *produced*, not
   that they were *plausible*.
7. **Spec drift in statistics.** Plan §4.3 says paired bootstrap
   (10,000 resamples). Code has `hmm::paired_ci`, a normal approximation
   over per-utterance diffs, with no bootstrap anywhere.

## 9. Operating rules for agents (add to AGENTS.md)

1. **Reuse first.** Before writing any new binary, script, loader or
   format, search for an existing one and cite it in the bead. A rewrite
   needs an architect sign-off line in the bead.
2. **Root cause before guard.** A failure bead must answer "is this the
   chain, the data, or the code?" with a measurement before proposing a
   fix.
3. **Plausibility gate** (§3) on every number before GREEN.
4. **Ceremony budget.** Seals and precommit apply to PHASE-TEST and
   LOCKBOX owner sessions only. DEV work needs a reproducible command,
   nothing more.
5. **No owner recording until the canonicalizer has QC'd a smoke
   session** from the same chain that day.
6. **Lanes don't edit outside their owned paths.** Interface changes go
   through the architect.
7. **The architect reviews every result doc before bead close**, with
   three questions: is the number plausible? Does the data support the
   claim? Did this move the critical path (R5a → demo)?
8. Keep OpenState/beads bookkeeping to what the next session needs; no
   bead renames or slug surgery during the hackathon.

## 10. Model / tooling recommendation

**Evidence (from this snapshot and its session record):**

- Strengths of the current agent (Muse Spark 1.3):
  - correct root-cause chain for the VOID run;
  - test-first discipline;
  - loud-failure instrument design;
  - honest relabeling;
  - clean A4 repair once the spec was frozen;
  - competent HMM/KN code.
- Weaknesses, all judgment-level:
  - rewrote the recorder twice despite an existing working one (owner
    had to intervene);
  - patched symptoms (silence gate, overflow truncation, `8lx`) instead
    of asking whether the capture chain changed;
  - closed beads GREEN on PER > 1, WER > 1, a 23-minute acoustic model,
    and a SCOTUS test for owner adaptation;
  - implemented a normal-approx CI where the plan said bootstrap;
  - wrote a gate requiring diagnostic ablations to pass (gh1 8/8);
  - shipped the A4 dormant-tiling defect, which only the cloud reviewer
    caught;
  - spent three days of owner time on a 30-word gate while the decoder
    was never started.

**Recommendation:** move the **architect / reviewer** role (bead writing,
interface freezing, result review, go/no-go) to the strongest model you
have access to. Keep Spark 1.3 for **lane execution** of beads written to
the §5 standard, where it performs well. The gear-grinding comes from the
decisions, not the typing, and a stronger reviewer costs far less than
another lost recording day. If budget forces one model, use the
stronger one for L4 (decoder) and for every result review.
